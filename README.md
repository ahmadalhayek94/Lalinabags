Terrace 2 — Japanese Garden Spa/Jacuzzi

Photorealistic architectural visualization of a Japanese-garden-inspired spa terrace, designed for tranquility and relaxation.

Layout & Space Planning
Terrace dimensions: 3.20m x 6.85m — a long, narrow space; design as a sequential journey from one end to the other rather than a single open zone.
Divide the terrace into two connected zones along its length:
Spa/jacuzzi zone: a built-in jacuzzi (round or softly rounded rectangular, sized to comfortably fit 2–4 people) set into a raised timber or stone deck platform, positioned toward one end of the terrace for privacy.
Tanning/sun lounge zone: 2 sun loungers positioned along the remaining length, angled to maximize both sun exposure and available floor width — consider a slightly recessed or stepped-down deck level to distinguish it from the spa zone while keeping an open, uninterrupted feel.
Optimize space tightly given the narrow footprint: use built-in bench seating/steps around the jacuzzi that double as plant ledges or storage, and keep circulation paths at a minimum comfortable width (0.8–1.0m) rather than wasting space on open walkways.
A low timber or stone screen/divider (partial height, not fully enclosing) can softly separate the jacuzzi zone from the lounging zone while preserving garden sightlines through it.
Materials
Decking: natural warm-toned wood decking (teak or thermally-treated timber) as the primary floor material, laid in clean linear boards to visually elongate the narrow space.
Stones: smooth river stones and larger flat natural stone slabs used as accents — stepping-stone paths, stone edging around the jacuzzi, and a small stone water feature or basin (tsukubai-style) if space allows near the entry.
Planting: curated Japanese garden planting — bamboo (clumping, non-invasive varieties) for vertical screening/privacy, Japanese maple or dwarf pine as a feature tree, moss or low ground-cover planting between stones, all in built-in planters to maximize the tight floor space.
Jacuzzi surround: natural stone or wood cladding on the jacuzzi shell to blend it into the timber deck rather than reading as a standalone tub.
Lighting
Soft stone lanterns (Japanese toro-style, in-ground or low profile) placed along the stepping path and near planting for gentle ambient glow.
Warm underwater/waterline lighting in the jacuzzi for evening use.
Subtle uplighting on the feature tree/bamboo to cast natural shadow patterns on the timber decking and travertine-style walls.
Low, warm lighting overall (2700K) to keep the mood calm, private, and spa-like rather than bright or clinical.
Style & Mood
Serene, minimalist Japanese zen garden aesthetic — natural materials, asymmetry, negative space (ma), and a sense of calm privacy.
Should feel like a private wellness retreat within the villa — quiet, textured, and sensory (sound of water, natural textures underfoot).
Materials and warm tones should still feel connected to the villa's overall palette (natural wood, stone, warm neutrals) without directly matching the pool terrace's microcement/travertine finish — this space reads as a deliberate contrast/retreat.
Camera Angle

Elongated view down the length of the terrace from the entry point, showing the stepping-stone path leading past the planting toward the jacuzzi, with the sun loungers visible to the side — daytime shot to emphasize natural light through bamboo, with a second dusk option showing the lantern and jacuzzi lighting glowing softly.

Terrace 3 & Rooftop Bar

Photorealistic architectural visualization of the upper-floor terrace, continuing the design language of the ground-floor pool terrace below.

Layout & Space Planning
Terrace dimensions: 8.90m x 4.10m, adjacent to the upper living/dining area and Master Bedroom.
This terrace sits directly above the ground-floor pool terrace — design the layout so the rooftop bar and seating are positioned to overlook the plunge pool below, with an open, unobstructed sightline down toward the pool (e.g., via a glass balustrade or open-edge design rather than solid railings).
Zone the space into two connected areas:
Rooftop bar: a compact built-in bar counter with a microcement finish, matching the resin/microcement tones used on the ground floor, including a small back-bar shelving/storage unit and 2–3 bar stools.
Cozy lounge seating area: a comfortable, low-slung outdoor sofa/daybed cluster with soft cushions, a low coffee table, positioned to take advantage of the pool view below and the open sky above.
Ensure a smooth, natural flow between the bar and lounge zones — no awkward pinch points, circulation width of at least 1.0–1.2m maintained throughout.
Important: the space currently shown in front of the Master Bedroom is being reclaimed to enlarge the Master Bedroom's interior footprint — do NOT include this area as part of Terrace 3; treat the terrace boundary as starting from the Master Bedroom's new (reduced) exterior wall line.
Materials
Flooring & bar counter: seamless microcement finish, matching the warm sand/greige tone from the ground-floor terrace for full material continuity top to bottom.
Walls/backdrop (where present): same mud-plaster tadelakt finish with a natural Roman-travertine look as the ground floor, carried upward for visual consistency.
Seating: natural woven textures (rope, rattan, or teak frames) with plush neutral-toned cushions (ivory, sand, warm taupe) for a relaxed, resort-style comfort.
Planting: minimal, curated planting in built-in microcement planters — olive trees or tall grasses at the perimeter to soften the edge without blocking the pool view.
Lighting
Warm pendant or hanging lantern-style lighting over the bar counter.
Low ambient lighting within the lounge seating (e.g., built-in bench lighting or low lanterns) for an intimate evening mood.
Uplighting on any travertine-textured walls to maintain the layered, warm-toned lighting language from the ground floor.
Subtle edge lighting along the open overlook edge (glass balustrade or terrace lip) so the pool below is visible and glowing at night, reinforcing the vertical connection between the two terraces.
Style & Mood
Continuation of the modern Mediterranean / boutique resort aesthetic from the ground floor — same material palette, same warm minimalist restraint.
The space should feel like a natural upward extension of the pool terrace: same tones, same materials, same lighting warmth — just elevated, airier, and framed by open sky.
Camera Angle

Wide shot from the rooftop bar/lounge area looking down and across toward the pool terrace below, capturing the vertical relationship between the two levels, at dusk to showcase the lighting design on both floors simultaneously.
